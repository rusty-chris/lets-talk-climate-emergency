Dear Secretary,

I am writing to request written permission to use IPCC Sixth Assessment Report material in a free, non-commercial educational chatbot, *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency*, which I steward through my consultancy, Rusty Data. The product is an educational public-benefit service: free to use, carrying no advertising, selling nothing; its code is published under the Apache-2.0 licence and its evaluation results are public. Its purpose is to give members of the public plain, accurate answers about the state of the climate, grounded strictly in authoritative publications, with every claim cited and linked to the source.

**What we are asking permission for.** We would like to build a private full-text retrieval index of the AR6 corpus — the Synthesis Report, the Summaries for Policymakers, the Technical Summaries, and the underlying Working Group chapters. We are asking because we recognise that indexing is itself a reproduction of the whole work, even though users never see the full text. What users *would* see is limited to: short verbatim excerpts (retrieved passages relevant to their question), each displayed unaltered with full acknowledgement (report, working group, section and page) and a deep link to the source document on ipcc.ch. Calibrated uncertainty language ("very likely", "high confidence") is preserved verbatim by design. The full text is never displayed, redistributed, or included in our public code repository; licensed material is held only in the private index.

**Our record-keeping.** Every document in our corpus carries a manifest record of its licence, the evidence for it, its permitted context of use, and a named human sign-off; the software refuses to index any document without this. Your reply would be filed as the permission evidence for the AR6 material, and any conditions you attach would be recorded and enforced there. Should the project ever cease to be non-commercial, material licensed on that basis is flagged for removal.

**An interim question.** Pending your decision on the fuller request, we intend to rely on the IPCC's published allowance for limited short excerpts with full acknowledgement, in one narrow form: a hand-curated set of **no more than ten headline statements per Summary for Policymakers**, each displayed verbatim with complete acknowledgement, and held behind a feature flag so the set can be disabled immediately at your request. Could you confirm whether this interim use is acceptable while the fuller permission is considered? If it is not, we will disable it.

A short reply confirming (a) permission to index AR6 full text for retrieval with short-excerpt display and deep links as described, and (b) whether the interim curated headline-statements set is acceptable in the meantime, is all we need; we are equally glad to complete any form or provide any further detail your procedure requires.
