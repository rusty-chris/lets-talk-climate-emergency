Dear Permissions Team,

I am writing to request permission for a specific, limited reuse of the following BioScience papers, published by Oxford University Press on behalf of the American Institute of Biological Sciences:

- Ripple et al., "World Scientists' Warning of a Climate Emergency", *BioScience* 70(1) (published online 2019; DOI: 10.1093/biosci/biz088), and
- the annual "World Scientists' Warning of a Climate Emergency" / "State of the Climate" update reports in *BioScience* (2021 onward, including future annual editions).

**The requester and the project.** I am Chris McWilliams, steward — via my consultancy Rusty Data — of *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency*, a free, non-commercial educational chatbot: no charge to users, no advertising, nothing sold; code published under Apache-2.0; every answer grounded in authoritative publications with inline citations. We are aware these papers are free to read but published under OUP's standard all-rights-reserved terms, which is why we are asking rather than assuming.

**What we are asking permission for.** To include the full text of these papers in a private retrieval index so the chatbot can find the relevant passages when users ask questions. We want to be straightforward about the mechanism: building a full-text index is a reproduction of the work, even though the index is private and the full text is never shown to users. What users see is limited to short verbatim excerpts — the retrieved passages relevant to their question — each displayed with full attribution (authors, title, journal, DOI) and a link to the article on academic.oup.com. The full text is never displayed, redistributed, or committed to our public repository.

**Why we hope this is an easy yes.** The papers themselves state their purpose: to warn the public plainly about the climate emergency. This project exists to carry exactly that warning to people who have not heard it, with the scientists' own calibrated words quoted verbatim and every reader one click from the original article. We are copying the lead author, Professor William Ripple of the Alliance of World Scientists, on this request as a courtesy, and would welcome any steer from OUP on whether author or AIBS consent should form part of the permission.

**Our record-keeping.** Every document in our corpus carries a manifest record of licence, evidence, permitted context, and named human sign-off; the software refuses to index anything without it. Your reply, and any conditions in it, would be filed as the permission evidence for these papers and enforced in configuration. If the project ever ceased to be non-commercial, material permitted on a non-commercial basis is flagged for removal.

A short reply confirming that we may index these papers for retrieval with short-excerpt display as described — or telling us what you need from us to get there (we are glad to pay a reasonable non-commercial licence fee if one applies) — is all we need.

With thanks for your time,
