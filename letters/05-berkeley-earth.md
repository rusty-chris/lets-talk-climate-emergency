# Letter 5 — Berkeley Earth (non-commercial confirmation)

> **Sending note (not part of the letter):** send to permissions@berkeleyearth.org — Berkeley
> Earth's stated contact for permissions and licensing (they list admin@berkeleyearth.org for
> commercial licensing, which this is not).

**To:** permissions@berkeleyearth.org

**Subject:** Request for written confirmation — non-commercial use of Berkeley Earth data and text under CC BY-NC 4.0 in a free educational chatbot

---

Dear Berkeley Earth team,

I am writing to ask for a short written confirmation about our planned use of Berkeley Earth material under your CC BY-NC 4.0 licence — we believe the use qualifies as non-commercial, and we would like your confirmation on record rather than relying only on our own reading.

**Who we are.** I am Chris McWilliams, steward — via my consultancy Rusty Data — of *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency*, a free educational chatbot that answers the public's climate questions strictly from authoritative sources, with every claim cited and linked to the original. The project is non-commercial in the plainest sense: free to use, no advertising, nothing sold, code open source under Apache-2.0, evaluation results public. Rusty Data may point to the project as work it has done, but the product itself earns nothing.

**What we would do with your material.** Two things, both attributed to Berkeley Earth with links:

1. **Text:** include selected Berkeley Earth analyses and explainers in a private retrieval index so the chatbot can find relevant passages. We are explicit that indexing is a reproduction of the work — which is why we are asking. Users never see the full text; they see short verbatim excerpts of the retrieved passages, credited and linked to berkeleyearth.org.
2. **Data:** fetch Berkeley Earth temperature datasets at run time, by script, for analysis behind cited answers.

Equally important is what we will **not** do: Berkeley Earth data and text are never committed to our public Apache-2.0 repository (they are fetched by script, so your licence terms always travel with the source), and they are deliberately **excluded from our downloadable chart-image data pack** — exported charts travel into contexts we cannot control, including commercial ones, and we will not put your NC licence at risk downstream.

Every document and dataset we use carries a manifest record of licence, evidence, permitted context and human sign-off, and your reply would be filed there as the permission evidence. If the project ever ceased to be non-commercial, all Berkeley Earth material is flagged for automatic removal.

**The ask:** could you confirm in writing that this use — retrieval indexing of Berkeley Earth text with short verbatim-excerpt display, and scripted use of Berkeley Earth data behind cited answers, in this free non-commercial educational product — qualifies as non-commercial use under CC BY-NC 4.0 as you apply it? A one-line reply is entirely sufficient, and if you would like conditions attached we will follow them.

With thanks for your work and your time,

Chris McWilliams
