Dear Carbon Brief team,

I am writing to ask for a short written confirmation about our planned use of your articles under your CC BY-NC-ND licence — we believe the use qualifies, and we would like your confirmation on record rather than relying only on our own reading.

**Who we are.** I am Chris McWilliams, steward — via my consultancy Rusty Data — of *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency*, a free educational chatbot that answers the public's climate questions strictly from authoritative sources, with every claim cited and linked. The project is non-commercial in the plainest sense: free to use, no advertising, nothing sold, code open source under Apache-2.0, evaluation results public. Rusty Data may point to the project as work it has done, but the product itself earns nothing.

**What we would do with your articles.** We would include selected Carbon Brief explainers and analyses in a private retrieval index, so the chatbot can find the relevant passages for a user's question. We are being explicit that this indexing is a reproduction of the articles — that is precisely why we want your confirmation that it falls within your non-commercial allowance. What users see respects both the NC and the ND terms:

- **Display is verbatim only:** short unadapted excerpts of your text, credited "Carbon Brief" with a link to the article, exactly per the terms on your site.
- **No derivatives:** the chatbot's own answer text either quotes you verbatim or states bare facts; we specifically test our system (with automated evaluations) to catch close paraphrase of your expression, which we treat as a licence failure.
- **No redistribution:** your articles are never republished in full, never included in our public code repository, and Carbon Brief content is excluded from anything users can export and reshare (such as our generated chart images).

Every indexed document carries a manifest record of licence, evidence, permitted context and human sign-off, and your reply would be filed there as the permission evidence. If the project ever ceased to be non-commercial, all Carbon Brief content is flagged for automatic removal from the corpus.

**The ask:** could you confirm in writing that indexing Carbon Brief articles for retrieval, with verbatim-excerpt display, credit and link as described, in this free non-commercial educational product, qualifies as non-commercial use under your licence terms? A one-line reply is entirely sufficient; and if you would rather we didn't, or would like conditions attached, we will follow whatever you say.

With thanks — and with appreciation for your work, which is exactly the kind of clear public climate communication this project exists to point people to,

Chris McWilliams
