Dear ECMWF Communication Section (Copernicus Team), and Chair of the WMO Publications Board,

I am writing to request permission for a specific, limited reuse of the **European State of the Climate (ESOTC)** reports produced by the Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S) — the most recent edition and, if you are willing, past and future annual editions.

**Requester (for your records).** Chris McWilliams, of the consultancy Rusty Data, acting as steward of *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency* — a free, non-commercial educational chatbot: no charge, no advertising, nothing sold; code published under Apache-2.0. Contact: tophermcw@gmail.com; website: https://github.com/rusty-chris/lets-talk-climate-emergency (project site to follow at [project URL when live]); postal address: [Chris's postal address].

**Why I am asking.** We note that the ESOTC report copyright page states that the right of publication is reserved by ECMWF and WMO and that "short extracts from this publication may be reproduced without authorisation, provided that the complete source is clearly indicated." Because the reuse we need goes beyond a short extract, we are asking rather than assuming — the same discipline we apply to every source in the project.

**Exact material and purpose.** We would like to include the ESOTC summary/report text in a private retrieval index, so that when a member of the public asks the chatbot a question, it can locate the relevant passages. Full-text indexing is a reproduction of the whole publication, beyond the short-extract allowance on your copyright page, even though the index is private and users never see the full text. What users see is limited to short verbatim excerpts of the retrieved passages, each displayed unaltered with full acknowledgement — we would attribute every excerpt as **"Generated using Copernicus Climate Change Service information [year]"** together with the report title, edition and section, and a link to the report on climate.copernicus.eu — and, per the joint publication, acknowledging WMO. The reports are never displayed in full, never redistributed, and never included in our public code repository.

**A narrower question alongside the request.** We understand ECMWF's website terms place ECMWF web content that carries no other rights notice under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence, scoped to the ecmwf.int domain. Could you tell us whether the ESOTC **web summary pages** (climate.copernicus.eu/esotc) fall under those CC BY web terms? If they do, we would index those pages under CC BY with the attribution above and would not need the broader permission for the summary content — we simply want to be certain of the scope before treating any climate.copernicus.eu page as openly licensed, rather than stretching the ecmwf.int terms ourselves.

**Our record-keeping.** Every document in our corpus carries a manifest record of licence, evidence, permitted context of use, and a named human sign-off; the software refuses to index anything without it. Your reply, and any conditions in it, would be filed as the permission evidence for ESOTC and enforced in configuration. If the project ever ceased to be non-commercial, material permitted on a non-commercial basis is flagged for removal.

A short reply confirming that we may index the European State of the Climate for retrieval with short-excerpt display and the Copernicus attribution described — and your answer on whether the ESOTC web summary pages fall under ECMWF's CC BY web terms — is all we need. If a formal request form is preferred at either organisation, I am glad to submit it there as well.

Yours faithfully,

Chris McWilliams
Rusty Data — steward, *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency*
