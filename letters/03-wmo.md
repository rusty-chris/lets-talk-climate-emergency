# Letter 3 — WMO (Publications Board)

> **Sending note (not part of the letter):** send to publications@wmo.int. WMO's copyright page
> states requests must identify the exact material, the purpose, and include name, organisation,
> contact details and website, and warns it "will not consider insufficiently documented requests" —
> the letter below covers each required element. WMO also has an online permission request form
> (wmo.int/copyright/permission-request-form-publications); offer to complete it, or complete it
> in parallel with the same text.

**To:** publications@wmo.int

**Subject:** Permission request — State of the Global Climate reports, retrieval indexing for a free non-commercial educational chatbot

---

Dear Chairperson of the Publications Board,

I am writing to request permission for a specific, limited reuse of the WMO **State of the Global Climate** reports (all published annual editions, and future editions as they appear).

**Requester (for your records).** Chris McWilliams, of the consultancy Rusty Data, acting as steward of *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency* — a free, non-commercial educational chatbot: no charge, no advertising, nothing sold; code published under Apache-2.0. Contact: tophermcw@gmail.com; website: https://github.com/rusty-chris/lets-talk-climate-emergency (project site to follow at [project URL when live]); postal address: [Chris's postal address].

**Exact material and purpose.** We would like to include the full text of the State of the Global Climate reports in a private retrieval index, so that when a member of the public asks the chatbot a question, it can locate the relevant passages. We are asking for permission because we recognise that full-text indexing is a reproduction of the whole publication, beyond the short-excerpt allowance on your copyright page — even though the index is private and users never see the full text. What users see is limited to short verbatim excerpts of the retrieved passages, each displayed unaltered with full acknowledgement (report title, edition, WMO publication number, section) and a link to the publication on wmo.int or library.wmo.int. The reports are never displayed in full, never redistributed, and never included in our public code repository.

**A specific question alongside the request.** We understand that the copyright notices printed in individual editions vary, and that some editions may carry their own non-commercial reuse allowance on the copyright page. Could you tell us which editions of the State of the Global Climate, if any, carry such a notice — or should we treat the terms on wmo.int/copyright as governing for all editions? We will follow whichever answer you give per edition.

**Our record-keeping.** Every document in our corpus carries a manifest record of licence, evidence, permitted context of use, and a named human sign-off; the software refuses to index anything without it. Your reply, and any conditions in it, would be filed as the permission evidence for these reports and enforced in configuration. If the project ever ceased to be non-commercial, material permitted on a non-commercial basis is flagged for removal.

A short reply confirming that we may index the State of the Global Climate reports for retrieval with short-excerpt display and links as described — together with your answer on the per-edition copyright notices — is all we need. If you would prefer the request via your online permission form, I am glad to submit it there as well.

Yours faithfully,

Chris McWilliams
Rusty Data — steward, *Let's Talk About the Climate Emergency*
